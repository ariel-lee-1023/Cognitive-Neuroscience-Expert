## Goal and diagnosis

By the end, learners should decode a small set of unfamiliar symbols; the main success criterion is accurate decoding after a delay. Immediate recognition can reflect perceptual familiarity, visible clues, or short-lived memory. It does not establish durable learning. First distinguish recognizing a symbol, recalling its sound, and combining symbols into an unfamiliar word: these are different tasks.

Assume an alphabetic script with six new letter–sound mappings. For a syllabic or other writing system, teach the corresponding functional units instead. Keep the initial set small enough to allow successful independent practice.

## A 30-minute lesson

| Time | Activity | Why and what to observe |
|---|---|---|
| 0–3 min | Give a short, ungraded baseline: show the six symbols individually and ask for their sounds. Explain that pauses and errors during practice are useful information. | Establish prior knowledge and reduce pressure to perform immediately. |
| 3–8 min | Explicitly teach three mappings. Say each sound, highlight its distinctive visual features, and contrast easily confused symbols side by side. Learners point to the feature and say the sound. | Direct attention to the features that distinguish symbols, rather than to incidental appearance. Avoid introducing many similar symbols without comparison. |
| 8–12 min | Hide the answers. Present symbols in shuffled order; everyone attempts the sound before receiving feedback. Revisit errors after intervening items. Once responses are mostly correct, use simple combinations. | Require retrieval, not copying or echoing. Feedback supplies the correct mapping; a later attempt checks whether the learner can now retrieve it. |
| 12–17 min | Teach the next three mappings with the same procedure, briefly retrieving earlier items between examples. | Separate repetitions in time and integrate old and new material. |
| 17–22 min | Mix all six symbols. Alternate identifying a symbol’s sound with selecting or writing the symbol for a spoken sound when relevant to the goal. Decode new combinations using only taught mappings. Introduce a second clear font after basic recognition succeeds. | Test flexible access and transfer. Writing is useful for a writing goal, but should not substitute for decoding practice. |
| 22–25 min | Take a brief break or do an unrelated verbal activity without displaying the symbols. | Remove immediate repetition and create a short retention interval. |
| 25–30 min | Give an individual, closed-material exit test, followed by correction and one fresh attempt at missed items. Explain the follow-up practice schedule. | Separate independent performance from assisted practice. Record the first test response before feedback. |

Allow several seconds for each retrieval attempt. If learners repeatedly fail, briefly show the mapping and simplify the next attempt; do not turn practice into prolonged guessing. If retrieval becomes effortless, lengthen the interval or add new combinations.

## Assess durable learning

Use parallel tests immediately, the next day, and about one week later. Include:

- **Retention:** taught symbols presented alone in a new order, without answer choices.
- **Discrimination:** confusable symbols, with errors recorded by symbol pair.
- **Transfer:** untaught combinations and a readable unfamiliar font, scored separately from practiced items.

Record accuracy first and response time second; speed alone can reward guessing. Report individual performance and which mappings remain unstable. Specify a practical criterion beforehand—for example, at least 90% symbol accuracy the next day plus successful decoding of new combinations—and adjust it to the intended use.

Schedule brief retrieval sessions after one day and several days, with feedback after attempts. These are starting intervals, not universal optima. Earlier follow-up tests themselves provide retrieval practice, so document them when interpreting week-later results. To evaluate the redesigned lesson causally, compare it with the old lesson using comparable learners, equal practice time, and the same delayed tests.
