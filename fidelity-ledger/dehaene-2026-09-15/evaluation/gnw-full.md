**No. It can challenge a specific GNW prediction, but posterior decoding plus weak frontal activity does not by itself decide between theories.** The crucial questions are whether the content was consciously experienced, what “ignition” measured, and which competing predictions were specified in advance.

### What the result establishes

Imagine a participant viewing faces and houses without pressing a response button. A classifier identifies the stimulus category from posterior cortex. This establishes that the recorded activity contains category information detectable by the classifier. It does **not** establish that the participant consciously experienced that information, or that it was available for flexible reasoning, memory, and action. GNW already permits substantial local processing without conscious access.

“Report-free” removes an overt reporting requirement, which helps separate perception from decision and motor preparation. It does not automatically hold attention, task relevance, memory, or covert reporting constant. Awareness needs converging evidence: for example, an independently validated percept-linked measure and calibration under comparable conditions. Occasional probes can help, but introduce expectations; delayed reports introduce forgetting. These tradeoffs should remain visible.

### Which GNW claim is at stake?

In Dehaene’s account, **conscious access** involves recurrent amplification and broad availability of a selected representation. Prefrontal involvement is part of the proposed neuronal implementation; a large frontal signal is not the definition of the entire mechanism.

There are therefore different possible conclusions:

- **An absent P3b:** challenges its use as an invariant awareness marker. P3b, frontal ignition, and global information sharing are different measurements or claims.
- **A reliable absence of a prespecified frontal ignition response during well-established conscious perception:** counts against the GNW version requiring that response in those circumstances. It should not be dismissed merely by renaming an undetected process “broadcasting.”
- **Preserved conscious content despite absence or selective disruption of the predicted distributed communication:** would challenge the mechanism more directly, provided the measures and intervention actually target it.

“Little” activity needs an effect estimate and uncertainty. A nonsignificant result can reflect limited recording coverage, poor sensitivity, an unsuitable time window, or variable trial timing. If ignition is inferred from EEG, overlapping components, filtering, reference choice, and latency jitter can diminish a peak; scalp topography alone cannot uniquely localize its generators. Conversely, adequate sensitivity and a tight bound excluding the predicted effect make a null result informative.

### Why this does not award victory to a rival

A posterior-recurrence account may accommodate conscious content without frontal ignition, but ordinary posterior decoding does not demonstrate the recurrent operation it proposes. Likewise, decoding from a posterior region does not establish IIT’s claims about causal organization. A finding compatible with several accounts has limited discriminating power; an anatomical location is not a complete mechanism.

**My synthesis for a stronger test:** preregister the exact versions of each theory, their timing and anatomical commitments, and the smallest response that would satisfy each prediction. Vary visibility and reporting demands separately while measuring attention and sensory differences. Test content-specific persistence and communication, not just classification accuracy. Where feasible, add appropriately controlled perturbations that distinguish a proposed contribution to experience from effects on attention, memory, or reporting. A selective effect must be interpreted against those alternative pathways.

The justified conclusion is therefore conditional: **this pattern favors separating conscious content from some frontal activity associated with task performance, and may disconfirm a specified frontal-ignition prediction. It does not settle GNW versus all alternatives.**
