# Coverage and fidelity — Dehaene extension

## Reading standard and limitations

The suite was frozen after conversion and bibliographic/TOC inspection, before semantic extraction. Reading used source-local line spans and later character-bounded paragraphs. Long unwrapped prose caused output truncation in early batches; the reading ledger retains those attempted emissions separately. Smaller probes recovered answer-changing material. Coverage is sampled structural coverage, not a claim of full-book reading or verification of every cited experiment. Line coverage overestimates paragraph coverage when only a character slice was inspected. Token values are script-density estimates, not billed model tokens.

All four supplied files extracted. The Number Sense contains hundreds of table-shaped OCR fragments; text-mode Markdown preserves the available text, and switching PDF extractors cannot recover image pixels absent from this input. Place-value notation was reconstructed; damaged subtraction diagrams and exact timings/clinical thresholds were not silently repaired. Original books, extraction corpus, screenshots, and temporary source slices are not published.

## Attribution decisions

Book terminology and chapter structure are retained in synthesized prose. The delta rule, logarithmic identity, task-control procedures, instructional design suggestions, and technological-mediation bridge are explicitly reconstructions or editorial integrations with the pre-existing collection. The seven learning headings are names/formulations, not copied expository passages. Worked examples are compact reconstructions with evidence limits, not reproduced datasets or figures.

The references remain below their source-based budgets; no padding was added to reach a minimum. Named-item density is not treated as an acceptance score for these scientific arguments: multi-step inference and qualifications take more space than a term list. Source locators and conditions were retained over shortening for a provisional prose-density heuristic.
