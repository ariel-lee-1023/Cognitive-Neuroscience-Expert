# Consciousness and the Brain: Deciphering How the Brain Codes Our Thoughts — Stanislas Dehaene

**Source edition:** Viking, 2014. Locators use the supplied book's chapter and subsection titles. This reference reconstructs Dehaene's global neuronal workspace account; its theoretical commitments and historical findings are not a consensus definition of experience. Later evidence is explicitly separated below.

## Mental Model (read first)
Dehaene approaches consciousness through **conscious access**: the transition by which a representation becomes available for flexible use by otherwise specialized systems. Much perception, valuation, and action preparation can occur without that transition. His **global neuronal workspace (GNW)** hypothesis explains access through recurrent amplification and long-distance sharing, with limited competition for entry. It proposes a mechanism and discriminating predictions; calling an activation “global” does not establish that mechanism or resolve every question about subjective experience.

**Use for:** masking and attentional-blink experiments, conscious versus unconscious processing, ignition and candidate neural signatures, access/report distinctions, and the evidential logic of testing noncommunicating organisms. Pair ERP claims with [Luck's technique reference](reference-luck-erp-technique.md) and the [component handbook](reference-kappenman-luck-erp-components.md); pair claims about an actual mechanism with [Craver](reference-craver-explaining-brain.md).

## Frameworks & Structure

### Chapter 5 — Theorizing consciousness: the proposed mechanism
**Locator:** “Consciousness Is Global Information Sharing,” “An Evolved Communication Network,” “Sculpting a Conscious Thought,” “Simulating a Conscious Ignition,” “The Restless Brain.”

**Global workspace versus specialized processors.** Many local systems process information in parallel. A workspace makes selected content available across systems involved in memory, evaluation, planning, and action. Baars's functional global-workspace proposal and Dehaene's neuronal implementation are related, but a functional architecture is not automatically evidence for a particular anatomical implementation. “Global” means widely available across relevant systems, not that every neuron participates or every area contains an identical copy.

**Global ignition** is the proposed nonlinear transition from a transient sensory response to a self-amplifying distributed representation. In the book's account, bottom-up input recruits higher areas; sufficiently strong recurrent feedback sustains and amplifies the representation. Competition limits which content is broadcast. The mechanism depends on timing, connection strength, background activity, and goals; there is no universal stimulus duration at which every person becomes conscious.

**Long-distance pyramidal connections** supply a candidate substrate for communication between association regions, including prefrontal, parietal, and cingulate areas. Recurrent interactions distinguish the proposal from a one-way sensory cascade. The network's organization matters more than locating a single “consciousness center.” Feedforward discrimination and recurrent global availability can produce different behavioral capacities despite sharing early sensory responses.

**Simulation logic:** a brief input in the book's simplified thalamocortical network produces activity that dies out; a stronger or longer input can trigger sustained reentry. A first ignition can temporarily prevent a second one, offering a model of a central bottleneck. Spontaneous activity makes otherwise identical inputs succeed or fail on different trials. These simulations establish consequences under their assumptions, not that a toy network has feelings or that the model is the brain's actual implementation.

**Task-directed prediction:** manipulate signal strength and attentional availability separately. Measure the early response, later amplification, persistence, and availability for a novel response or delayed use. Ask whether a competing local-recurrence or report-related account predicts the same contrast. A nonlinear curve alone is insufficient: sensory thresholds and decisions can also be nonlinear.

### Chapter 1 — Consciousness enters the lab: define the contrast
**Locator:** “The Many Facets of Consciousness,” “Minimal Contrasts,” “Rival Images,” “When Attention Blinks,” “Masking Conscious Perception,” “Primacy of the Subjective.”

**Conscious access, selective attention, vigilance, and metacognition** answer different questions. Access concerns which content becomes available. Selective attention prioritizes information and can act without awareness. Vigilance concerns the enabling state of the system; wakefulness does not establish awareness of a particular object. Self-consciousness concerns representations of oneself, while metacognition evaluates one's own cognitive activity. A confidence judgment is not identical to perceptual accuracy. These distinctions prevent a task measuring one capacity from silently becoming a test of them all.

**Minimal contrast:** compare conditions with nearly matched sensory input but different reported awareness. Near-threshold masking, binocular rivalry, and the attentional blink supply different interventions, with different confounds. In rivalry, the physical input can remain constant while perception alternates. In masking, visibility depends on target–mask timing and strength. In the blink, attending a first target reduces report of a second within a task-dependent interval. The paradigms should not be treated as interchangeable manipulations of a single uncontaminated variable.

**How to construct an informative experiment:** characterize the visibility function within participants; verify display timing; measure discrimination and subjective visibility; vary attention or reporting independently where feasible; compare physically matched trials; inspect response bias, memory, and selection. Reports are indispensable evidence about experience, but they also recruit operations that a percept alone may not require. Post hoc sorting of “seen” and “unseen” trials can select different internal states; it is not itself randomized assignment to consciousness.

**Subjective and objective measures:** a participant's “unseen” report and chance-level forced choice are distinct observations. Failure to reject chance is not proof of zero sensitivity. Use a task capable of detecting the information relevant to the purported unconscious effect, with adequate precision and comparable stimulus conditions. Delayed questioning risks forgetting; repeated questioning changes expectations. These are design tradeoffs, not reasons to discard either first-person report or performance.

### Chapter 2 — Fathoming unconscious depths: identify what was processed
**Locator:** “Binding Without Consciousness,” “Unconscious Meaning?,” “The Great Unconscious Wars,” “Unconscious Arithmetic,” “Attentive but Unconscious,” “A Subliminal Bag of Tricks.”

**Subliminal priming** occurs when a poorly perceived prime changes responses to a subsequent target. The informative question is which representation mediates the change: visual features, letter identity/order, lexical or semantic information, a practiced category, or a motor response. Cross-case repetition can constrain a purely pictorial explanation. Priming by a rearranged letter string differs from priming by the same ordered word, showing that some feature binding need not await consciousness.

**Learned response mappings** can mimic semantic understanding. If each prime has repeatedly been associated with a response, faster congruent responses need not establish flexible access to its meaning. Novel items, changed mappings, and transfer can distinguish explanations. A positive effect should travel with its actual control conditions; a small average effect may otherwise reflect occasional visibility or a subgroup with residual sensitivity.

**Attention without awareness:** attentional selection can amplify an invisible stimulus or its priming effect. Therefore, “unconscious” does not mean attention-free, automatic under every condition, or immune to goals. Likewise, evidence for unconscious discrimination does not establish unconscious multistep planning, open-ended reasoning, or unlimited learning. The book's ambitious examples vary in evidential strength and do not license a single list of faculties proved unconscious.

### Chapter 3 — What is consciousness good for?
**Locator:** “Unconscious Statistics, Conscious Sampling,” “Lasting Thoughts,” “The Human Turing Machine,” “A Social Sharing Device.”

Dehaene emphasizes a **compact conscious synthesis** of information that can be held stable, routed to different operations, and shared with other people. Such a synthesis trades detail and parallelism for flexible availability. It explains why the consciously experienced interpretation can appear definite despite competing and probabilistic processes underneath.

**Lasting thoughts:** access supports maintaining and manipulating information after its source disappears. Contrast transient priming with an explicitly retained intermediate result. This is a proposed functional advantage, not a claim that unconscious learning cannot leave durable synaptic effects or that every form of working memory requires continuous reported awareness.

**The human Turing machine:** conscious control can arrange otherwise specialized operations into novel serial sequences. In an unfamiliar calculation, maintaining an intermediate result and choosing the next rule differ from retrieving an overlearned answer. A bottleneck prediction should specify the operations competing for shared resources; “people cannot do two things” is too broad because peripheral processes and practiced routines can run concurrently.

**Social sharing:** language and gesture make a compressed mental result available to others, allowing distributed problem solving and comparison of confidence. This is an evolutionary/functional proposal, not proof that language is necessary for experience or that social cooperation uniquely identifies conscious processing.

### Chapter 4 — The signatures of a conscious thought
**Locator:** “The Avalanche of Consciousness,” “Timing the Conscious Avalanche,” “Igniting the Conscious Brain,” “Deep Inside the Conscious Brain,” “The Brain Web,” “Decoding a Conscious Thought.”

The book groups evidence around **four candidate signatures**: a late amplification involving distributed association cortex; a late positive ERP often called P3/P3b in this literature; sustained high-frequency activity; and long-distance synchronization/information exchange. They are measured at different scales and should not be conflated into four independent confirmations of one cause. Source timing varies by paradigm; the book's roughly 200–400 ms transitions are observations in particular tasks, not a universal clock of experience.

**Measurement-to-mechanism chain:** a scalp positive wave is not a direct recording of a workspace. High-frequency power is not identical to phase synchronization. Correlated activity is not sufficient to establish directed broadcasting. Decoding content shows information detectable by an analyst, not necessarily flexible information use by the participant. Trial averaging can hide variable latencies or create an apparently smooth transition from discrete events.

**Necessary, sufficient, and correlated** are different claims. Lesions, stimulation, anesthetic changes, and recording convergence constrain them differently. A null response may result from inadequate sensitivity or changed task demands; a positive response may be a prerequisite or a consequence of awareness. Specificity needs appropriate alternatives, including attention, memory, decision, and report.

### Chapter 5 — A catalog of the unconscious
**Locator:** “A Catalog of the Unconscious,” “Subjective States of Matter.”

| Why information is unavailable in the book's GNW taxonomy | Consequence for testing |
|---|---|
| **Preconscious:** represented strongly enough but excluded by inattention or competition | Shifting attention may permit access before the representation decays. |
| **Subliminal:** input is too weak, brief, or disrupted for ignition under the tested conditions | More attention need not rescue it; change target/mask conditions. |
| **Disconnected patterns:** a processor lacks the relevant route to the workspace | Strong local activity alone does not imply access; test communication or another representational route. |
| **Diluted/implicit patterns:** information is present in a code unavailable to workspace readout | Analyst decoding can outperform introspection without revealing conscious content. |
| **Latent connections:** knowledge persists as synaptic organization rather than an active explicit representation | Retrieval must reactivate content; being unable to verbalize a learned rule does not mean no learning occurred. |

This is a theory-guided taxonomy, not five clinical diagnoses. Do not identify which bin a person occupies from one failed report. “Preconscious” here has a specific processing meaning and does not import a psychoanalytic theory.

### Chapter 6 — The ultimate test: inference when behavior is limited
**Locator:** “How to Lose Your Mind,” “Cortico Ergo Sum,” “Freeing the Inner Butterfly,” “Conscious Novelty Detection,” “Pinging the Cortex.”

The book distinguishes coma, wakeful unresponsiveness/vegetative-state presentations, minimally conscious states, and locked-in syndrome. The methodological lesson is that **motor output, arousal, comprehension, and awareness can dissociate**. Its historical terminology and diagnostic details are not a current clinical protocol.

**Command-following imagery:** changing between imagined tennis and spatial navigation in response to an instruction can provide evidence of intentional task performance despite absent overt movement. A positive, reproducible result supports retained capacities; a negative result is inconclusive because hearing, language, attention, fatigue, task comprehension, and measurement sensitivity can fail. It does not independently quantify pain, prognosis, all conscious content, or treatment preferences.

**Local–global auditory regularities:** a five-tone sequence can contain a locally deviant last sound while being the globally common sequence. After that pattern is learned, a rare all-identical sequence can violate the global expectation without a within-sequence local mismatch. This dissociates levels of regularity. Early mismatch responses and a later global effect support different inferences; attention and task relevance matter. Counting global deviants strengthens the late response, itself a reason to examine task dependence.

**Perturb and observe:** TMS–EEG probes whether a perturbation evokes a differentiated, extended response rather than a short local one. This aims at the system's capacity for integrated activity without requiring an ordinary verbal response. An index of response complexity is not itself a report of experience, and impressive classification within a study does not supply universal sensitivity, specificity, or a bedside threshold. For an actual patient, retrieve current clinical evidence and involve the treating team.

### Chapter 7 — The future of consciousness
**Locator:** “Conscious Babies?,” “Conscious Animals?,” “Self-Conscious Monkeys?,” “Conscious Machines?”

Use converging behavior, developmentally appropriate tasks, and neural evidence when verbal reports are unavailable. Similar masking/rivalry patterns and flexible nonverbal reports can strengthen cross-species comparisons. Anatomical homology or mirror recognition alone does not establish the full character of experience. Adult timing thresholds cannot simply be applied to infants.

Dehaene treats self-monitoring and confidence as possible extensions of accessible representations. He argues that a sufficiently organized artificial system could in principle be conscious, while acknowledging the gap between his simulations and such a system. Block's access/phenomenality distinction and Chalmers's explanatory challenge remain genuine disagreements. Explain what GNW claims to explain and what critics think is left out; do not present the book's response as a resolved philosophical theorem.

## Later evidence and theory boundaries

These additions are external checks, not passages from the 2014 book.

- **P3b and reporting:** Cohen, Ortego, Kyroudis & Pitts (2020), *Distinguishing the Neural Correlates of Perceptual Awareness and Postperceptual Processing*, report a dissociation between awareness and P3b under changed reporting demands. This challenges using P3b as an invariant consciousness marker; it does not by itself refute every cognitive theory. No-report designs still require evidence that the relevant content was experienced and must assess changes in attention or memory. [Primary abstract and figure descriptions](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32409620/).
- **Adversarial theory test:** the COGITATE Consortium (2025) preregistered contrasting GNWT/IIT predictions and tested 256 participants with fMRI, MEG, and intracranial EEG. Its results challenged both theories, including GNWT's predicted prefrontal offset ignition and IIT's sustained posterior synchronization prediction. This constrains the tested biological predictions; it is not a test resolving every version of either theory or the philosophical nature of experience. [Primary article](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-08888-1).

When discussing newer findings, reopen the primary paper and its limitations. A fixed supplement checked in September 2026 is not an exhaustive account of all subsequent research.

## Worked Example
**Reconstruction from Chapter 6, “Conscious Novelty Detection.”** Repeatedly present a common sequence A-A-A-A-B. The final B violates the immediate acoustic repetition, but the five-sound sequence becomes expected. Occasionally present A-A-A-A-A: it contains no local acoustic change, yet violates the sequence-level regularity. Compare the early local response with the later global response, using appropriate sequence controls.

If a patient has an early mismatch response alone, infer preserved processing of an acoustic regularity, not confirmed conscious comprehension. If a reproducible global response appears, it supports a broader processing capacity under those conditions; command following, repeated assessments, and clinical context provide additional constraints. Failure to detect it does not establish absence of experience. The later P3b literature makes this asymmetry especially important.

## Decision Rules & Judgment

- Start by stating whether the target is access, experienced content, vigilance, self-monitoring, or communication; do not let one stand in for the others.
- For an unconscious-processing claim, name the processed feature and rule out residual visibility and learned response shortcuts relevant to that feature.
- For GNW, connect recurrence, competition, persistence, and flexible availability to a discriminating prediction; naming frontal activity is insufficient.
- For a candidate signature, separate awareness from report, task relevance, memory, and attention. Pair the inference with the appropriate measurement reference.
- For absent behavior, assess the output channel before inferring absent awareness. Positive and negative command-following results have asymmetric evidential force.
- For theory comparison, distinguish the core claim from an auxiliary anatomical/timing prediction and say which one a result challenges.
- For AI, infants, or another species, transfer a hypothesis with explicit measurement assumptions; do not transfer an adult marker as an established consciousness test.

## Key Takeaways

1. The book's explanatory target is primarily conscious access, supported by a proposed neuronal mechanism.
2. Specialized processing can be elaborate without flexible conscious availability.
3. Candidate signatures need task and measurement controls, and later evidence qualifies their universality.
4. Failure to communicate is not sufficient evidence of unconsciousness.
5. Reproducing a functional workspace does not settle whether a system has subjective experience.

**Coverage note:** All seven chapters contribute to the reference. Historical vignettes, exhaustive priming examples, detailed molecular/anesthetic pathways, individual-patient prognoses, and speculative engineering timelines are compressed or omitted. No present-day clinical thresholds or AI-consciousness verdicts are derived from the book.
